“A honeypot detects attacks that target the services it intentionally exposes, and may adapt or rotate its infrastructure to increase coverage and realism.”

“A multi‑service honeypot exposes multiple decoy services, such as SSH, HTTP, and FTP, on a single system to simultaneously detect diverse application‑level attack behaviors.”

“A honeypot is a network-accessible decoy system that exposes services to capture attacker behavior. While it exists on the network, it is designed to detect interactions with these services (application-level and behavioral attacks) and does not inherently detect attacks targeting the network or protocol itself.”

“A honeypot is network-accessible to allow attackers to reach it, but it is designed to detect attacks that interact with the exposed services (application and behavioral level) rather than attacks that operate purely at the network/protocol level.”

OUR PROPOSAL SYSTEM LIMITATION
“Our honeypot system is designed to detect attacks that interact with exposed services, primarily at the application and behavioral levels. Pure network-level attacks, such as SYN floods, UDP amplification, or ICMP floods, do not involve meaningful interaction with services and therefore cannot be detected effectively by a traditional honeypot.”

EXPLANATION
Network-level attacks are volume or protocol based, not behavior-based.
Honeypots rely on interaction and engagement to reveal attacker intent.
Detecting network floods requires traffic analysis, rate-limiting, or anomaly detection, not deception.

ACCOUNT
“To account for network-level attacks, our system could be integrated with network-based monitoring tools such as IDS/IPS, NetFlow analysis, or darknets, which can capture anomalous traffic patterns, volumetric attacks, and protocol-level abuses. This complements the honeypot’s application-level and behavioral coverage.”

OUR SCOPE
“While our adaptive honeypot focuses on application-layer and behavioral threats, pure network-based attacks are out of its direct detection scope. They can be monitored using complementary network security tools, such as IDS/IPS or traffic anomaly detection systems, allowing a combined proactive defense strategy.”

Basically,
Both — honeypots can exist at the network level and the application level.
It depends on what you want to observe or lure.

🌐 Network‑level honeypots

Designed to look like vulnerable hosts or network services.

What they imitate:

Open ports (SSH, FTP, Telnet, SMB)

Weak network services

Entire fake machines

Examples:

Cowrie (SSH/Telnet)

Dionaea (malware collection)

Honeyd

OSI layers: mostly L2–L4
Purpose: detect scans, worms, brute-force attacks

🧠 Application‑level honeypots

Designed to look like real applications.

What they imitate:

Web apps

APIs

Login pages

Admin panels

Examples:

Glastopf (web app attacks)

WordPress honeypots

Fake REST APIs

OSI layers: L7 (Application)
Purpose: capture SQLi, XSS, CSRF, auth abuse

🧩 Quick summary
Honeypot type	Lives at	Catches
Network honeypot	Network / transport	Scans, brute force, malware
Application honeypot	Application	Web & API attacks
